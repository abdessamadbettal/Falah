Malik-ul-Mulk is the Master of the Kingdom — who owns all dominion and gives or withdraws it as He wills.

## What the name means

The phrase joins owner to owned: not a king within a kingdom, but the owner of kingship itself. What rulers hold is on issue from Him.

The verse spells out the transfer in both directions: You give sovereignty to whom You will and take it from whom You will.

## Living by this name

It is the Quran's answer to political despair and to political arrogance at once. Power changes hands by decree, which means no arrangement is permanent.

Its response is to hold whatever authority you have lightly. See also [[al-malik]] and [[al-qahhar]].
