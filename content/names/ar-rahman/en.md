Ar-Rahman is the mercy that arrives before it is asked for — the mercy that reaches every creature alive, believer and disbeliever alike, simply because Allah is merciful.

## What the name means

Ar-Rahman comes from the root r-h-m, the same root as rahim, the womb — a word Arabic uses for the place where a life is sheltered and fed before it can ask for anything. The form rahman is one of intensity and scope: not merely one who shows mercy, but one whose mercy is vast, immediate and overflowing.

It is the only one of the ninety-nine that Arabic reserves for Allah alone. A man may be called karim or halim, but never rahman, and the Quran pairs it with the throne and with the act of teaching the Quran itself, as though to say that everything given to creation comes from this attribute first.

## Ar-Rahman and Ar-Rahim

The two names open every surah together, and scholars have long read them as distinct rather than repeated. Ar-Rahman is mercy in its breadth — the sun that rises on everyone, the rain that falls on every field. Ar-Rahim is mercy in its depth and its constancy, the mercy Allah keeps for the believers in this life and the next.

Read that way, the basmala says something exact: the One whose mercy reaches all, and who is especially merciful to those who turn to Him.

## Living by this name

A servant cannot share in this name, but can respond to it. The Prophet ﷺ said that Allah shows mercy to those who show mercy to others, and tied the treatment of people on earth to the mercy received from above.

Practically it means beginning with mercy rather than judgement — with your family, with those who have wronged you, with yourself when you have sinned and assume the door is shut. Ar-Rahman is the answer to that assumption.
