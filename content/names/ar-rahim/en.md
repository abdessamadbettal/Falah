Ar-Rahim is mercy that stays — the particular, enduring mercy Allah keeps for those who believe, in this world and the next.

## What the name means

Ar-Rahim shares its root with Ar-Rahman, but the form differs. Where rahman describes mercy overflowing in the moment, rahim describes mercy as a settled, permanent attribute — mercy that does not exhaust itself and does not withdraw.

The Quran uses it more than any other of the ninety-nine, most often paired with forgiveness: ghafurun rahim, the Forgiving, the Merciful. The pairing is deliberate. Forgiveness removes the debt; mercy gives what was never owed.

## Mercy that is specific

Allah describes Himself as rahim bil-mu'minin — merciful to the believers — a specificity Ar-Rahman does not carry. Scholars read this as the mercy of the hereafter: the general mercy of this world reaches everyone, while this one is kept for those who answered.

It is also the name applied to the Prophet ﷺ in the Quran, where he is called ra'uf rahim toward the believers — the only creature described with a form of this attribute.

## Living by this name

The believer's share is to be reliably merciful rather than occasionally kind: mercy as a settled habit, not a mood that passes.

It is also the name to hold on to when despair argues that the door has closed. The verse says: tell My servants that I am the Forgiving, the Merciful. The invitation is stated in the same breath as the attribute.
