Al-Hamid is the Praiseworthy — the One who deserves all praise, whether or not anyone offers it.

## What the name means

Hamd is praise joined to love, offered for what someone is as much as for what they give. Allah is Hamid in the passive sense — deserving of it — and His praiseworthiness does not depend on being recognised.

The Quran pairs it with self-sufficiency: Allah is al-Ghaniyy, al-Hamid — needing nothing, and praiseworthy regardless.

## Living by this name

It explains why the Quran opens with praise rather than with a request. Recognition comes before asking.

Its response is alhamdulillah in both conditions — the Prophet ﷺ praised Allah in what pleased him and in what did not. See also [[ash-shakur]] and [[al-ghaniyy]].
